1. How to use this manual
Only have 5 minutes before class? Read The screen inside a screen and follow the Two-monitor recipe. The rest is for reference.

Zoom updates every month, so some names may look slightly different on your screen.

Convention: button names appear in bold, exactly as Zoom shows them in English. Example: Share.

3. The screen inside a screen
In a nutshell It happens because Zoom is capturing itself: you are sharing the same monitor where the meeting is open, like two mirrors facing each other.
Fix: share the monitor where Zoom is not, or share a single window.

Check these causes in order; the first one that matches is usually yours.

What you seeLikely causeFix
The Zoom window repeated in a tunnel, smaller and smallerYou shared the entire screen of the monitor where the meeting is openShare the other monitor or a specific window. See the recipe.
The same thing, even though it never happened beforeThe option to show your Zoom windows is turned onSettings → Share Screen → uncheck Show my Zoom windows to other participants when I am screen sharing. Restart Zoom.
Both monitors show exactly the same thingWindows is set to DuplicatePress Windows + P and choose Extend.
You see your own class, small, inside another windowThe meeting is open twice (browser or another device)Move that second window to the monitor you are not sharing, or close it.

If nothing matches, take a screenshot with Windows + Shift + S during class and send it to tech support.

4. Recipe: screen sharing with two monitors
Golden rule One monitor for teaching, the other for Zoom. That way there is never a mirror effect, and you can see your students and the chat while you explain.

Before class (one time only)

  1. Press Windows + P and make sure it is set to Extend.
  2. Decide which one is your teaching monitor (for example, the right one) and which one is your Zoom monitor (the left one).

Every class

  1. Open what you will show (Brightspace, MyOpenMath, PowerPoint, GeoGebra) and drag it to the teaching monitor.
  2. Join the meeting and keep Zoom on the Zoom monitor. Open Participants and Chat there.
  3. Click Share on the bottom toolbar.
  4. Choose the Screen 2 thumbnail, or whichever matches your teaching monitor. Zoom shows a large number on each monitor to identify it.
  5. If you will play a video, check Share sound and Optimize for video clip.
  6. Click Share. A green border shows what students can see.
  7. Work on the teaching monitor; watch your students on the Zoom monitor.
  8. To switch content: New Share. To finish: Stop Share.
Safer option Share only a window (in step 4, choose the program's thumbnail). That way nobody sees your email or notifications. To share a single Chrome or Edge tab, look in the Other section.

With PowerPoint: go to Slide Show → Set Up Slide Show and choose Browsed by an individual (window). The presentation then stays in a window you can share on its own.

6. Annotate and the Whiteboard
In a nutshell The tool to select and move what you wrote still exists: it is the arrow, the first icon on the annotation toolbar. What is new is that the Annotate button may be hidden under More.

A. Writing on your shared screen

  1. Share your screen or a window (see the recipe).
  2. On the floating toolbar at the top, click Annotate (the pencil icon). If you don't see it, open it from More (the three dots).
  3. The annotation toolbar appears. Choose Text or Draw and write.
  4. To move what you wrote, click the arrow: Select. Click on what you wrote (or drag a box around several items) and drag it.
  5. When you finish, close the annotation toolbar. Annotations are erased when you stop sharing, so save them first if you need them.

B. The annotation toolbar, icon by icon

ToolWhat it doesIdea for math class

The order or look of the icons may vary slightly depending on your Zoom version.

C. Letting students write too

  • Go to Host tools → Allow all participants to → Annotate to turn it on or off (see Host tools).
  • On the floating toolbar, More → Show Names of Annotators shows who wrote each thing.
  • If the Annotate option does not appear anywhere, it may be turned off in your institution's account. Check with City Colleges tech support.

D. The Whiteboard

  1. Click Share and, in the Other section, choose Whiteboard.
  2. In the new whiteboard, the toolbar is on the left side. You can drag it elsewhere on the screen.
  3. The arrow to select and move is also the first icon on that toolbar.

The new whiteboard includes tools that help in math:

  • Equation: writes nicely formatted formulas (fractions, exponents, roots).
  • Text and drawing: the same functions as annotation, with more shapes.
  • Upload: places an image or PDF on the whiteboard so you can write on top of it (for example, a GED exercise).
  • Copy and paste, sticky notes and templates.
  • Handwriting conversion: turns what you wrote with the mouse or pen into clean text and shapes, with a preview before you accept.
Important difference What you write on the shared screen disappears when you stop sharing. What you write on the Whiteboard is saved in your Zoom account, and you can open it in another class.

7. Toolbar, settings and self view

A shorter toolbar

Only the essential buttons remain at the bottom. Everything else is under More (the three dots). To pin a button you always use, such as Breakout Rooms or Record: open it under More, right-click → Pin. You can also drag buttons to reorder them.

Settings in a single panel

Settings open in a panel on the right. The small arrow ^ next to each button takes you straight to that button's settings.

Floating self view (new)

Your video has a button with two options:

  • Float over gallery: your video becomes a small box you can move around.
  • Show in gallery: your video goes back to its place among the others.

If it gets in the way, choose Show in gallery or Hide self view. To change the default, go to Settings → Meetings & webinars → Self view defaults.

Light or dark mode

The whole meeting now follows your appearance preference: Settings → General → Appearance.

9. Other new features useful for class

These features fit Universal Design for Learning (UDL) well, especially with multilingual students:

  • Chat translation: each participant can see the chat translated into their language.
  • Voice translator: participants hear the class translated as audio (Spanish, English, French, Chinese, Japanese, Arabic, German, Italian, Portuguese). May depend on your institution's plan.
  • Pin a chat message: the host pins, for example, the MyOpenMath link, and it stays at the top of the chat, even for latecomers.
  • Captions and transcripts separated: you can turn on captions without creating a transcript, or the other way around.
  • Early-join notice: Zoom lets you know when the first student joins before class time.
  • Breakout rooms: students in the waiting room can go straight to their assigned room.

10. Attendance reports
In a nutshell At CCC, attendance for online classes is taken with the Zoom participant report. You download it from the Zoom web portal as a CSV, save it as Excel (.xlsx), and upload it to the attendance folder in SharePoint (Teachers → your name).

The report is not taken from the desktop app but from the web portal: zoom.us. Sign in with your CCC account. Wait a few minutes after class ends; the report does not always appear right away.

A. Getting to the reports (new web portal)

The Zoom web portal was also redesigned in 2026. The left-hand menu is no longer visible from the home page.

  1. Go to zoom.us and sign in with your CCC account.
  2. Click your picture or initials at the top right. The profile menu opens.
  3. Choose My Account.
  4. In the left-hand menu, click Reports.
  5. On the Usage Reports tab, choose Usage.
Shortcut Bookmark the Reports page once you reach it. Next time you go straight there, without the menus.

B. Finding the class and downloading participants

  1. Enter the From and To dates and click Search. Each search covers one month at most. You can also search by meeting ID.
  2. Find the row for your class (topic, date and time).
  3. In the Participants column, click the number. A window opens with the list: name, email, join time, leave time and minutes.
  4. Check the boxes:
    • Export with meeting data: adds the class topic, date and ID above the list, so the file identifies itself.
    • Show unique users: combines a student who disconnected and rejoined into a single row and adds up their minutes.
  5. Click Export. A .csv file downloads to your Downloads folder.

C. Converting the CSV to Excel

  1. Open the .csv file with Excel (double-click, or right-click → Open with → Excel).
  2. Check that names with accents or ñ display correctly (José, Muñoz). If you see strange symbols, see the note below.
  3. Optional: adjust column widths (double-click the line between column letters) and sort by name.
  4. Click File → Save As. Under Save as type, choose Excel Workbook (*.xlsx).
  5. Give it a clear name, for example: Attendance_MATH-ABE-01_2026-10-01.xlsx (group and date).
If accents show up as strange symbols In a blank Excel workbook, go to Data → From Text/CSV, choose the file and, under File Origin, select 65001: Unicode (UTF-8). Click Load, then save as .xlsx.

D. Uploading the Excel file to SharePoint

The attendance folder is in SharePoint → Teachers → your name.

  1. Open your browser and go to office.com (or CCC's Microsoft 365 portal) with your @ccc.edu account.
  2. Open SharePoint from the app launcher (the nine dots, top left).
  3. Go to the Teachers site or library.
  4. Open the folder with your name. If there is a subfolder for each group, open the one for that class.
  5. Click Upload → Files and choose the .xlsx from your Downloads folder. You can also drag the file straight into the window.
  6. Check that the file appears in the list with the correct name and date.
Shortcut Once you are inside the folder with your name, add it to your browser's bookmarks. Next time you get there in one click, without going through office.com or Teachers.

E. Tips for a clean report

  • Ask students to join with their real name and, if possible, their account. “María's iPhone” makes attendance harder. You can rename them from Participants → More → Rename.
  • Always use the same link or meeting ID for each group, so it is easy to find in the report.
  • The Duration (minutes) column shows who arrived late or left early.
  • If the Reports option does not appear, your account may not have permission. Check with City Colleges tech support.

11. Checklist and common problems

Before every class (2 minutes)

  • Windows + P is set to Extend.
  • Materials open on the teaching monitor.
  • Zoom, chat and participants on the Zoom monitor.
  • Notifications silenced (Windows: Do not disturb).
  • Zoom up to date: profile picture → Check for updates.

After every class

  • Download the participant report (section 10).
  • Save it as Excel with the group name and date.
  • Upload it to SharePoint → Teachers → your folder.

Common problems

ProblemQuick fix
Mirror or tunnel effectShare the other monitor or a single window (section 3).
I can't find the arrow to move what I wroteAnnotate (on the floating toolbar or under More) → first icon, Select (section 6).
I can't find a buttonLook under More; pin it with a right-click.
My video covers the contentSelf view button → Show in gallery or Hide self view.
Students can't hear the videoNew Share and check Share sound.
A student can't shareHost tools → Who can share? → All participants.
I can't find the reportsWeb portal zoom.us → profile picture → My Account → Reports → Usage (section 10).
Everything looks different from this manualCheck your version under Help → About Zoom.
